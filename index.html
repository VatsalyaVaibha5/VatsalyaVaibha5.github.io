Research Interests

Quantum Gravity and Cosmology

The Λ-CDM model has been extremely successful in explaining the underpinning mechanism of the Universe. It is remarkable how it is so consistent with another extremely successful model which is the Standard Model of particle physics. Together, these two models have the potential to explain the entire cosmic history of the Universe but it is the general consensus that the standard story is incomplete and we need a different cosmological epoch at very early times. I am interested in the minimalistic possibility that the radiation dominated epoch with just the Standard Model particles (and right-handed neutrinos) extends all the way to the Big Bang under appropriate boundary conditions and quantum gravity effects to answer the cosmological puzzles. To investigate this possibility further, I have been working with quadratic gravity which is a minimalistic UV completion of gravity and have also thoroughly studied the asymptotic symmetries of power law cosmologies to understand cosmological perturbation theory completely at a non-linear order and look for experimental signatures in the form of cosmological memory effects.

Lattice Fermions

Lattice field theory provides the only reliable handle to deal with strongly coupled quantum field theories and has been extremely succesful in understanding the infrared regime of QCD. However, one of the last standing problems of lattice field theory is to simulate a chiral guage theory such as the Standard Model. At the heart of this problem is the fermion doubling problem along with no-go theorems that prohibit a lattice description of chiral fermions. Along with my supervisor Latham Boyle, I have been thinking about the Kahler-Driac fermions as a possible route to overcome this issue. Wilsonian discretization for bosonic fields is extremely successful because all the continuum fields are discretized according to their Lorentz representation, i.e., scalars are placed at the vertices, vectors on the links, and rank-2 tensors on the plaquettes. We believe that ignorance in the understanding of spinor geometry is the core of the fermion doubling problem. Kahler-Dirac fermions provide a geometrical description of fermions in terms of polyforms and makes it possible to realize the appropriate discretization of fermions on the lattice while preserving the topological properties of the continuum manifold.

Mathematical Physics

One of the most important discoveries of physics in the 20th century was the deep relation between symmetries and conserved quantities. The Standard Model of particles physics can be explained using the direct-product of the simplest Lie groups. But why is the Standard Model what it is and where do the unexplained values of the fine-tuned parameters come from? At the same time, there are only four normed Division algebras that play a crucial role in the classification of compact simple Lie and Jordan algebras. Would it not be elegant if the Standard Model that describes the real world comes from the mathematics of only allowable algebras that follow the law of division? Another phenomenal coincidence with the Standard Model symmetries is that they are preserved under quantization (a symmetry is called anomalous otherwise). Anomalies are interesting mathematical objects that depend only on the topological properties of the twisted principle G-bundle. I have been studying the anomalies of the Standard Model and generic QFTs in various contexts such as on the lattice and under uplifting the theory to twistor space.
