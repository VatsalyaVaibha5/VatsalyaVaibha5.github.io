Research Interests

Quantum Gravity and Cosmology

The $\Lambda$-CDM model has been an extremely successful endeavor to understand the underpinning mechanism of the Universe. It is remarkable how it is so consistent with another extremely successful model which is the Standard Model of particle physics. Together, these two models have the potential to explain the entire cosmic history of the Universe but it is the general belief that at very early times they have to be replaced by a more fundamental theory of quantum gravity. I am interested in minimalistic models of quantum gravity such as quadratic gravity as potentials tweaks to the standard story that could explain some of the remaining conceptual puzzles to our understanding of the Universe while assuming that the standard radiation dominated universe extends all the way to the Big Bang.

Lattice Fermions

Lattice field theory provides the only reliable handle to deal with strongly coupled quantum field theories and has been extremely succesful in understanding the infrared regime of QCD. However, one of the last standing problems of lattice field theory is to simulate a chiral guage theory such as the Standard Model. At the heart of this problem is the fermion doubling problem along with no-go theorems that prohibit a lattice description of chiral fermions. Along with my supervisor Latham Boyle, I have been thinking about the Kahler-Driac fermions as a possible route to overcome this issue. Unlike usual fermions, Kahler-Dirac fermions provide a geometrical description of fermions in terms of polyforms and makes it possible to realize the appropriate discretization of fermions on the lattice while preserving the topological properties of the continuum manifold.

Mathematical Physics

One of the most important discoveries of physics in the 20th century was the deep relation between symmetries and conserved quantities. The Standard Model of particles physics can be explained using the direct-product of the simplest Lie groups. However, the quantum description of the theory may not respect the classical symmetries. This phenomenon of violation of the symmetry due to quantization of the theory is called anomaly. Anomalies are interesting mathematical objects that depend only on the topological properties of the twisted principle G-bundle. I have been investigating the anomalies that appear in lattice models with Kahler-Dirac fermions and the Weyl anomaly using the exact renormalization group (ERG) and twistor methods. Previously, I have tried to understand the underlying mathematical structures of the Standard Model using Division algebras and the exceptional Lie groups.
